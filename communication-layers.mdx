<Card title="Definition" type="note" horizontal>
  "Social Penetration Theory" = the idea that relationships develop as people gradually share more about themselves, moving from surface-level information to more personal thoughts and feelings. It was introduced by psychologists Irwin Altman and Dalmas A. Taylor in 1973 in their book _Social Penetration: The Development of Interpersonal Relationships_. 
</Card>

## The Onion Model

### Outer Layers

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Outer Layers" = the superficial, public level of communication in which people share observable characteristics and safe, basic information, such as their appearance, hobbies, role, or general preferences.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card type="check">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### Middle Layers

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Middle Layers" = the moderately personal level of communication in which people share opinions, preferences, goals, attitudes, and everyday experiences after a basic level of trust has developed.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card type="check">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### Inner Layers

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Inner Layers" = the private and emotionally significant level of communication in which people disclose personal values, fears, sensitive experiences, close relationships, and feelings not usually shared with strangers.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card type="check">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### Core Layers

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Core Layers" = the deepest and most protected level of communication in which people reveal their central identity, fundamental beliefs, deeply held values, strongest emotions, and most vulnerable aspects of themselves.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card type="check">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### Breadth Dimension

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Breadth Dimension" = the range and number of different topics, areas of life, or personal categories that people discuss and disclose during communication.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card type="check">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### Depth Dimension

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Depth Dimension" = the level of intimacy, personal meaning, and emotional sensitivity of information shared, from general public facts to deeply private thoughts and feelings.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card type="check">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

## Stages of Depth

### Stage 1: Orientation

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Orientation" = the initial stage of communication in which strangers exchange only safe, impersonal, and socially expected information while cautiously forming first impressions.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card type="check">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### Stage 2: Exploratory Exchange

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Exploratory Exchange" = the stage in which communication becomes more relaxed and friendly, allowing people to share casual opinions, preferences, and limited personal information.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card type="check">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### Stage 3: Affective Exchange

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Affective Exchange" = the stage in which people communicate more openly by expressing genuine views, personal feelings, and more emotionally meaningful information.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card type="check">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### Stage 4: Stable Exchange

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Stable Exchange" = the stage in which people communicate openly, personally, and comfortably, with a strong understanding of each other’s thoughts, feelings, and usual behavior
    </Card>
  </Column>

    </Card>
