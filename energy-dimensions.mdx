## Physical Energy

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note" icon="🤓">
      "Physical Energy" = a human's capacity to feel alert and awake, move with strength, stamina, and coordination, carry out daily physical tasks, and recover from exertion.
    </Card>

    <Card title="How we manage this energy:">
      - We offer flexible hours so people can work with their natural energy rhythms.
      - We subsidize gym access so movement doesn't compete with a packed schedule.
      - We provide healthy food options so people aren't skipping meals to keep working.
      - We set clear boundaries on after-hours availability to protect sleep.
      - We encourage walking meetings and standing desks to break up sedentary time.
      - We monitor chronic overtime as an early warning sign of burnout.
      - We design office space with light, comfort, and quiet in mind.
      - We train managers to spot early signs of physical exhaustion.
      - We protect lunch breaks as real, uninterrupted time.
      - We offer flexible return-to-work options after parental leave.
      - We provide ergonomic equipment by default, not on request.
      - We set deadlines that respect human limits, not just business goals.
      - We track workload distribution so exhaustion isn't concentrated unfairly.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card title="When a person has enough energy:" type="check" icon="😇">
      - Can stay awake and alert through most of the day.
      - Can handle physical tasks (stairs, errands, lifting, standing) without feeling drained.
      - Can sustain an activity for a suitable length of time and at an appropriate level.
      - Can notice when the body needs food, water, or rest.
      - Can recover quickly after exercise, exertion, or a bad night's sleep.
      - Can have enough physical capacity left for enjoyable activities.
      - Can maintain steady appetite and digestion.
      - Can heal and recover from illness or minor injury in a normal timeframe.
    </Card>

    <Card title="When a person lacks energy:" type="danger" icon="😨">
      - Can feel groggy or exhausted even after a full night's sleep.
      - Can find simple physical tasks (stairs, errands, lifting) feel disproportionately draining.
      - Can move stiffly, heavily, or with visible reluctance.
      - Can take a long time to recover after exercise, exertion, or poor sleep.
      - Can lose appetite or experience digestive discomfort.
      - Can take unusually long to heal or bounce back from illness or minor injury.
      - Can feel physically weak or shaky without clear cause.
      - Can rely heavily on caffeine or stimulants just to function.
      - Can experience frequent headaches or muscle tension from fatigue.
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

## Mental Energy

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note" icon="🤓">
      "Mental Energy" = a human's capacity to focus and sustain attention, process and retain information, plan ahead, solve problems, generate ideas, and make decisions.
    </Card>

    <Card title="How we manage this energy:">
      - We protect deep-work blocks by limiting meetings during certain hours.
      - We limit how many priorities any one team carries at once.
      - We default to async updates whenever a meeting isn't essential.
      - We simplify internal processes that quietly tax people's focus.
      - We favor single-tasking over multitasking in how we design work.
      - We build in recovery time after major launches or crunches.
      - We reduce decision fatigue on low-value choices like approvals.
      - We provide mentorship so people aren't stuck alone on hard problems.
      - We provide quiet spaces for people who need to think deeply.
      - We create clear handovers between shifts and teams.
      - We investigate recurring confusion as a possible work-design problem.
      - We rotate repetitive tasks so no one gets stuck in monotony.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card title="When a person has enough energy:" type="check" icon="😇">
      - Can focus on a task without constantly losing track of it.
      - Can follow a conversation or switch between tasks without losing the thread.
      - Can learn new information and retain it without excessive repetition.
      - Can think clearly under moderate pressure or ambiguity.
      - Can generate ideas and make connections fairly easily.
      - Can make decisions without excessive second-guessing or paralysis.
      - Can remember names, appointments, and details without heavy reliance on reminders.
      - Can follow complex conversations or instructions the first time.
      - Can solve problems methodically rather than feeling scattered.
    </Card>

    <Card title="When a person lacks energy:" type="danger" icon="😨">
      - Can struggle to focus, drifting off after only a few minutes.
      - Can find new information hard to absorb or retain.
      - Can feel foggy or slow, especially under any pressure.
      - Can lose the thread when switching between tasks.
      - Can feel blocked, unable to generate ideas or make connections.
      - Can freeze up or endlessly second-guess simple decisions.
      - Can forget names, appointments, or details more than usual.
      - Can feel scattered rather than methodical when solving problems.
      - Can lose track mid-sentence or mid-thought when speaking.
      - Can procrastinate more than usual, avoiding cognitive effort.
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

## Emotional Energy

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note" icon="🤓">
      "Emotional Energy" = a human’s capacity to notice, experience, express, and regulate feelings, cope with stress, and respond to other people’s emotions.
    </Card>

    <Card title="How we manage this energy:">
      - We train managers to recognize when someone is struggling.
      - We provide confidential counseling before stress becomes overwhelming.
      - We recognize contributions regularly, not just at review time.
      - We create safe channels to raise concerns without fear of retaliation.
      - We give people space to process hard news before expecting output.
      - We protect customer-facing staff from abusive interactions.
      - We check in on wellbeing, not just performance, in one-on-ones.
      - We treat stress as normal, not as a performance failure.
      - We survey morale regularly and act visibly on what we learn.
      - We offer flexibility during difficult personal situations when possible.
      - We share emotionally demanding work and provide backup.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card title="When a person has enough energy:" type="check" icon="😇">
      - Can experience an uncomfortable feeling without immediately pushing it away.
      - Can feel a normal range of emotion — not flattened, not constantly reactive.
      - Can express feelings appropriately instead of bottling them up or exploding.
      - Can recover from a bad mood or setback within a reasonable time.
      - Can tolerate criticism or conflict without becoming defensive or shutting down.
      - Can pause before reacting when upset.
      - Can empathize with someone else's pain without being overwhelmed by it.
      - Can set a boundary when an interaction becomes too demanding.
      - Can ask for support when needed rather than isolating.
    </Card>

    <Card title="When a person lacks energy:" type="danger" icon="😨">
      - Can feel numb or disconnected from their own feelings.
      - Can bottle up feelings or, alternately, snap and overreact.
      - Can grow impatient or irritable with others over small things.
      - Can feel overwhelmed by someone else's pain instead of able to hold space for it.
      - Can become defensive or shut down in response to criticism or conflict.
      - Can feel emotionally flat or, conversely, highly reactive and volatile.
      - Can struggle to comfort others because there's nothing left to give.
      - Can feel disconnected from people they're usually close to.
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

## Spiritual Energy

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note" icon="🤓">
      "Spiritual Energy" = a human's capacity to draw strength from a sense of meaning and purpose, stay connected to their values and beliefs, and remain motivated to act in ways that feel worthwhile, whether religious or not.
    </Card>

    <Card title="How we manage this energy:">
      - We connect individual roles back to the company's larger purpose.
      - We support personal values through volunteering and giving programs.
      - We never ask employees to act against their own ethics.
      - We give people room to shape their role around what feels meaningful.
      - We support career paths aligned with what people actually care about.
      - We hold leadership to the same values we ask of everyone else.
      - We offer internal mobility to people facing a crisis of meaning.
      - We offer sabbaticals so long-tenured people can reconnect with purpose.
      - We involve people in decisions that affect their work and community.
      - We respect commitments outside of work, not just inside it.
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card title="When a person has enough energy:" type="check" icon="😇">
      - Can connect everyday actions to something meaningful.
      - Can keep acting on their values even when it's inconvenient or unrewarded.
      - Can stay motivated to show up for responsibilities even when tired or discouraged.
      - Can hold a clear (even if evolving) sense of "why" behind major choices.
      - Can recover a sense of direction after setbacks, rather than feeling adrift.
      - Can find work, relationships, or effort worthwhile beyond just utility or duty.
      - Can make decisions that feel congruent with their identity, not just circumstance.
      - Can sustain effort on long-term goals even without daily emotional highs.
    </Card>

    <Card title="When a person lacks energy:" type="danger" icon="😨">
      - Can feel like daily actions are pointless or disconnected from anything that matters.
      - Can abandon their values when it's inconvenient or unrewarded to hold them.
      - Can feel a sense of purpose that depends entirely on external praise or results.
      - Can feel adrift or lost after a setback, unable to find direction again.
      - Can experience work, relationships, or effort as empty obligation rather than worthwhile.
      - Can make decisions that feel out of step with their own identity.
      - Can feel cynical or disillusioned about things they once cared about.
      - Can question whether anything they do actually matters.
      - Can feel a vague, persistent sense of emptiness or futility.
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>
