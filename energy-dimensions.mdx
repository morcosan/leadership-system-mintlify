## Physical Energy

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Physical Energy" = a human's capacity to feel alert and awake, move with strength, stamina, and coordination, carry out daily physical tasks, and recover from exertion.
    </Card>

    <Card icon="sparkles">
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card title="When a person has enough energy:" type="check">
      - Can stay awake and alert through most of the day.
      - Can handle physical tasks (stairs, errands, lifting, standing) without feeling drained.
      - Can sustain an activity for a suitable length of time and at an appropriate level.
      - Can notice when the body needs food, water, or rest.
      - Can recover quickly after exercise, exertion, or a bad night's sleep.
      - Can have enough physical capacity left for enjoyable activities.
      - Can maintain steady appetite and digestion.
      - Can heal and recover from illness or minor injury in a normal timeframe.
    </Card>

    <Card title="When a person lacks energy:" type="danger">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

## Mental Energy

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Mental Energy" = a human's capacity to focus and sustain attention, process and retain information, plan ahead, solve problems, generate ideas, and make decisions.
    </Card>

    <Card icon="sparkles">
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card title="When a person has enough energy:" type="check">
      - Can focus on a task without constantly losing track of it.
      - Can follow a conversation or switch between tasks without losing the thread.
      - Can learn new information and retain it without excessive repetition.
      - Can think clearly under moderate pressure or ambiguity.
      - Can generate ideas and make connections fairly easily.
      - Can make decisions without excessive second-guessing or paralysis.
      - Can remember names, appointments, and details without heavy reliance on reminders.
      - Can follow complex conversations or instructions the first time.
      - Can solve problems methodically rather than feeling scattered.
    </Card>

    <Card title="When a person lacks energy:" type="danger">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

## Emotional Energy

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Emotional Energy" = a human’s capacity to notice, experience, express, and regulate feelings, cope with stress, and respond to other people’s emotions.
    </Card>

    <Card icon="sparkles">
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card title="When a person has enough energy:" type="check">
      - Can experience an uncomfortable feeling without immediately pushing it away.
      - Can feel a normal range of emotion — not flattened, not constantly reactive.
      - Can express feelings appropriately instead of bottling them up or exploding.
      - Can recover from a bad mood or setback within a reasonable time.
      - Can tolerate criticism or conflict without becoming defensive or shutting down.
      - Can pause before reacting when upset.
      - Can empathize with someone else's pain without being overwhelmed by it.
      - Can set a boundary when an interaction becomes too demanding.
      - Can ask for support when needed rather than isolating.
    </Card>

    <Card title="When a person lacks energy:" type="danger">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>

## Spiritual Energy

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Card title="Definition" type="note">
      "Spiritual Energy" = a human's capacity to draw strength from a sense of meaning and purpose, stay connected to their values and beliefs, and remain motivated to act in ways that feel worthwhile, whether religious or not.
    </Card>

    <Card icon="sparkles">
    </Card>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Card title="When a person has enough energy:" type="check">
      - Can connect everyday actions to something meaningful.
      - Can keep acting on their values even when it's inconvenient or unrewarded.
      - Can stay motivated to show up for responsibilities even when tired or discouraged.
      - Can hold a clear (even if evolving) sense of "why" behind major choices.
      - Can recover a sense of direction after setbacks, rather than feeling adrift.
      - Can find work, relationships, or effort worthwhile beyond just utility or duty.
      - Can make decisions that feel congruent with their identity, not just circumstance.
      - Can sustain effort on long-term goals even without daily emotional highs.
    </Card>

    <Card title="When a person lacks energy:" type="danger">
    </Card>
  </Column>
</Columns>
